Quegan: infer leakage from unwanted correlations

In a reflection-symmetric calibration area, true co-pol and cross-pol returns are uncorrelated on average. Measured correlation reveals channel mixing.

The additional scene assumption

True HH/VV must not predict true cross-pol

⟨SHHSHV*⟩ = ⟨SVVSHV*⟩ = 0
Σ =
PH00D0PXPX00PXPX0D*00PV

Ideal noise-free covariance, also using reciprocity. The black zeros are ensemble averages; cross-pol power PX is not zero.

Correlating measured HV with HH and VV exposes their leaked contributions.

Solve for those leaked contributions

Two correlations → two leakage coefficients

C11C41C14C44
uv
=
C21C24

In the thesis convention: channels 1–4 are HH, HV, VH, VV. This small-cross-talk system separates the HH and VV contributions to HV; repeat for VH to obtain z and w.

Δ = C11C44 − |C14|²
u = (C44C21 − C41C24)/Δ

No iteration: invert the 2 × 2 system directly. It needs Δ sufficiently different from zero.

Then estimate cross-pol imbalance
X = C32 − zC12 − wC42   ;   αQ = (C22 − uC12 − vC42)/X

X is the leakage-corrected VH–HV correlation. Compare corrected HV power with that shared signal, form M, then apply M−1 to each pixel.

Why can it fail? Real scene correlations can be mistaken for leakage. At low cross-pol SNR, noise adds power without the same coherent contribution to X. Small |X| makes α unstable; one pass cannot rescue invalid assumptions.
