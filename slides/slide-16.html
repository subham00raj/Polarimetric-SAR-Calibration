Faraday rotation: untwist propagation, not the target

A linear wave is the sum of two opposite circular components. In the magnetized ionosphere they accumulate different phases: their recombination rotates the polarization plane.

Ideal trihedral S = I: two passes add to 2Ω. For other targets, use the full matrix model.

Ω = half the circular phase difference; magnitude scales with f−2 ∫neB∥ ds: electron density, field and path length.

Why two rotations?

The wave crosses the ionosphere outward and back. Faraday rotation is non-reciprocal: the return pass does not undo the outward pass.

M = F(Ω) S F(Ω)
F(Ω) =
cos Ωsin Ω−sin Ωcos Ω

Even when the target has SHV = SVH, the observed MHV and MVH can differ.

Ŝ = F(−Ω̂) M F(−Ω̂)

Remove one rotation on each side. The return factor is not transposed.

How your code estimates Ω

Transform to a circular basis: rotation becomes phase.

Z12 = (MHV − MVH) + i(MHH + MVV)
Z21 = (MVH − MHV) + i(MHH + MVV)
arg ⟨Z12Z21*⟩ = −4Ω
⇒ Ω̂ = −¼ arg ⟨Z12Z21*⟩

The opposite ±2Ω phases give a 4Ω difference. Average the complex product first; code default: 10 × 10 boxcar. Principal-angle ambiguity: |Ω| < 45°.
