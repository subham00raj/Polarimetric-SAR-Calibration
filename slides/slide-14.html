04 · Calibration models · Stage 3 of 4

Ainsworth: make two views of the same scattering agree

A reciprocal target has SHV = SVH. Different measured HV and VH responses therefore contain clues about gain imbalance, leakage and noise.

Start with the physics

One signal, two distorted measurements

OHV ≈ a1SHV + b1SHH + c1SVV + n1
OVH ≈ a2SHV + b2SHH + c2SVV + n2

First-order illustration: a terms scale the desired signal; b and c terms leak co-pol energy into it.

We do not know the true signal in any pixel. Average many pixels instead: C = ⟨o o†⟩, with o = [OHH, OHV, OVH, OVV]T.

Turn reciprocity into a statistical target

Match the corrected HV/VH statistics

Σ ≈
PHA*A*DAβ + ηβBAββ + ηBD*B*B*PV

Equal A and B entries encode the same HV/VH relationships to HH and VV. A and B may remain non-zero: real scene correlations are retained.

β is shared cross-pol power; η models equal, uncorrelated noise. Match statistics, not noisy pixel values.

What makes it different?

It retains real co-/cross-pol correlations. Reciprocity needs heuristic completion and normalization; absolute and co-pol gains still need independent calibration.
