02 · Why calibration

Why is calibration required?

How the calibration tone corrects channel phase → slide 13

Hardware and propagation effects

Amplitude imbalance: unequal H/V gains bias channel ratios and relative scattering strengths.

Relative phase bias: unequal electrical path lengths (T, A and R paths) shift the phase differences between polarization channels.

Cross-talk: imperfect isolation mixes H and V signals, contaminating weak cross-polarized returns.

Ionosphere (spaceborne SAR): Faraday rotation mixes H/V, especially at L/P-band. Dispersive delay and phase shifts also need propagation correction.

Noise also affects the measurement

Blackbody radiation: thermal radiation from the scene contributes to antenna noise through its brightness temperature.

Receiver thermal noise: random electronic fluctuations add noise and a positive bias to measured intensity.

Random errors: measurement fluctuations reduce SNR and the reliability of estimated calibration parameters.
