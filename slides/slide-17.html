04 · Interpreting calibrated PolSAR

Decomposition: from polarization to scattering mechanisms

The radar measures complex HH, HV, VH and VV. Their relative amplitudes, phases and correlations help us interpret the scattering mechanisms within each image cell.

Surface / odd bounce

A rough surface or plate: one dominant reflection. Ideal HH and VV are in phase.

Double bounce

Ground–wall or ground–trunk path: two reflections. Ideal HH and VV have opposite signs.

Volume

Many differently oriented scatterers, such as branches: a mixture of returns often produces cross-pol.

Helix

A handed, non-reflection-symmetric response; can occur in complex structures. It need not be a physical coil.

Pauli · recombine the complex channels

Add and subtract HH and VV before taking magnitudes. In-phase returns reinforce the sum; opposite-phase returns reinforce the difference.

kP = (1/√2) [HH + VV, HH − VV, 2HV]T

For reciprocal data, HV = VH. This is a coherent basis change, not a fitted mixture of physical powers.

Sum → surface-likeDifference → double-bounce-likeCross-pol → depolarizing response

RGB in your code: R = |HH − VV|, G = |HV + VH|/√2, B = |HH + VV|. Cross-pol is not uniquely volume scattering.

Yamaguchi · fit an average power mixture

One resolution cell can contain many scatterers. Average their channel correlations, then represent that covariance using four scattering models.

C = ⟨kLkL†⟩ ≈ ∑j ∈ {s,d,v,c} PjCj

kL = [HH, √2(HV + VH)/2, VV]T; model matrices Cj normalized to unit trace.

Span = tr(C) ≈ Ps + Pd + Pv + Pc

RGB: R = Pd, G = Pv, B = Ps; helix Pc is examined separately. These are model-dependent estimates, not unique ground-truth labels.

Why use this to check calibration? Gain and phase errors change sums, differences and correlations, creating apparent mechanism changes. Your code uses per-pixel Pauli amplitudes and a 5 × 5 averaged four-component estimate; RGB contrast is stretched separately and should not be read as power fractions.
